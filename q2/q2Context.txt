\section*{Q2 Trajectories, Optimization \& Selecting K}

\subsection*{Context}
You now have a per-session progress score for every client in the dataset. Taken together, these
scores form a progress trajectory for each child across care. Some children show strong gains
early and then level off. Others improve gradually. Others show uneven or delayed progress.

This question asks you to do three things in sequence: build a clustering model, derive an
optimal reassessment policy from the clusters it produces, and then use what you learn from
that policy to decide how many clusters to use.

\subsection*{(a) Build the Clustering Model}
Build a pipeline that converts per-session scores to a $0$--$3$ scale, constructs cumulative progress
trajectories across Sessions $1$--$12$, and applies K-means clustering for a value of $K$ that you supply.
Generate spaghetti plots showing individual trajectories within each cluster and the cluster mean.

\subsection*{(b) Derive the Optimal Reassessment Policy}
For each cluster, apply the newsvendor-style audit model below to the clusters you have built. This
is the prescriptive core of the question: given a cluster's distribution of stopping points, when
should Westfield schedule the reassessment?

\paragraph{Model setup}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{p{0.18\textwidth} p{0.72\textwidth}}
\toprule
\textbf{Parameter} & \textbf{Definition} \\
\midrule
$T_{\max}$ & Maximum sessions in the funded pathway $= 12$ \\[0.5em]
$t_i^*$ & Stopping point for client $i$: earliest session where cumulative progress
$\ge 90\%$ of that client's total cumulative progress across all sessions \\[0.5em]
$Q$ & Reassessment session chosen for a given cluster (the decision variable) \\[0.5em]
$F_c(Q)$ & Empirical CDF of $t^*$ within cluster $c$:
$F_c(Q) = P(t^* \le Q \mid c)$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\paragraph{One-shot audit rule (mirroring Westfield's re-assessment logic in Exhibit B):}
\begin{itemize}
    \item If $Q < t_i^*$: reassessment is too early; the child continues to $T_{\max}$ (no sessions saved)
    \item If $Q \ge t_i^*$: plateau confirmed; care ends at $Q$ $(T_{\max} - Q$ sessions saved)
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Expected sessions saved:}
\[
\mathbb{E}[\text{savings}](Q) = F_c(Q)\times (T_{\max} - Q)
\]

\paragraph{Objective:}
for each cluster $c$, find
\[
Q_c^* = \arg\max_Q \mathbb{E}[\text{savings}](Q)
\]

For each client, compute $t_i^*$ using the fraction-of-total-progress rule. Then, for each cluster and
for $Q = 1,2,\ldots,12$, compute $\mathbb{E}[\text{savings}](Q)$ and identify $Q_c^*$.
Do this for each value of $K$ you are evaluating.

\subsection*{(c) Select K}
Use the policy outcomes to commit to a final value of $K$. The goal is a clustering that produces
reassessment policies that are genuinely distinct and actionable.

State your final choice of $K$ and justify it in terms of both what the trajectory shapes look like and
what the optimization revealed.

\subsection*{(d) Required Plots for Your Final K}
Using your chosen $K$, produce the following three plots:

\paragraph{Plot 1 --- $t_i^*$ distributions.}
For each cluster, plot a histogram of $t_i^*$ values (one panel per cluster,
shared x-axis). The x-axis is the stopping session ($1$--$12$) and the y-axis is the count of clients. These
distributions show whether stopping points are concentrated or spread out within each group, and
whether they tend to fall early or late in the pathway.

\paragraph{Plot 2 --- Expected sessions saved vs.\ $Q$.}
For each cluster, plot $\mathbb{E}[\text{savings}](Q)$ against
$Q = 1,2,\ldots,12$ on a single set of axes (one line per cluster). Mark $Q_c^*$ for each cluster with a vertical
dashed line. This curve makes the tradeoff between auditing early and auditing late visible, and
shows why different clusters call for different reassessment timing.

\paragraph{Plot 3 --- Optimized $Q^*$ vs.\ mean($t^*$) baseline.}
For each cluster, compute a naive baseline
policy by rounding the mean $t^*$ within that cluster. Plot expected sessions saved per child side-by-side for the optimized $Q^*$ and the baseline (one grouped bar per cluster). Report the overall
difference in expected savings across all clients and explain intuitively why the optimized policy can
outperform the mean heuristic.
